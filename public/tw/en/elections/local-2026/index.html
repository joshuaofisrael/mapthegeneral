Who votes, and how

The Civil Servants Election and Recall Act gives suffrage to a citizen of the Republic of China who has reached the age of 20, and requires at least four consecutive months of residence in the electoral district. A person who moves into the district after the election notice has no suffrage for that election. A voter votes at the polling station for the registered domicile unless the Act provides otherwise. A worker at a station in the same municipality or county may vote at the station where they work or at the domicile station. The voter presents a national identification card to receive a ballot. A person must arrive during the hours specified for voting. Someone who arrives late is not admitted. Someone already inside may still vote. When several elections share a station, entry is once.

The report does not print an opening hour or a closing hour. This page does not supply one. No count is shown before 00:00 on 29 November 2026, Taiwan time. That instant is the start of the next calendar day. It is not a closing time the Commission printed.

The report says commissions had reported, by 30 April 2026, an expected 18,227 polling stations. It says that is 578 more than the 17,649 stations of the ROC year 111 local elections and constitutional referendum, and 395 more than the 17,832 stations of the ROC year 114 national referendum. The figure 18,227 is marked as expected. It is not a final list of stations.
