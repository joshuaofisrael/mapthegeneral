# Country onboarding assessment, 23 October 2026

This note is not legal advice. It is an editorial safety check for Map the General: whether a modest, sourced guide can be published without a tracker, without candidate or officeholder personal data, and without municipality mass pages. It does not clear any other use of the same material.

Primary statutes were not read article by article except where a section is named below. Where a fact comes from an authority’s own summary rather than the statute, that is said. Where a page could not be read, the fact is omitted and the gap is flagged. A 2023 European Parliament briefing on election silence was not used as current law.

Germany stays off the site. It was removed on 18 October 2026 for an open legal question and this note does not reopen it.

**Country scope.** Only first-world, high-income countries may be added. Do not onboard a country outside that set, even when a guide would otherwise pass the checks below.

## How a verdict was chosen

**Onboard now.** An official election authority page was read on 23 October 2026, the guide can stick to that page, there is no open blackout or regulated advertising period that a new election site would sit inside, and the hub will publish no polls, no tracker, no candidate names and no candidate photographs.

**Recommend only.** A guide may be possible later, but a primary rule was not read, or a live election restriction makes a new site not clearly low-risk.

**Skip.** The country was already removed for an unresolved election-law problem, or the restriction that is known is strict enough that a new hub should not be added on a partial reading.

## Onboard now

### Ireland

- **Silence and polls.** The Electoral Commission’s news item of 1 November 2024 says Coimisiún na Meán removed the 24-hour broadcast moratorium ahead of that year’s general election. That item is about broadcasters. Current broadcaster guidelines were not re-read on 23 October 2026. This site is not a broadcaster and will publish no polls. No statutory poll-publication ban for websites was read.
- **Political advertising.** The Commission’s political-advertising page describes the EU Transparency and Targeting of Political Advertising Regulation as applying to paid political advertising. This site publishes none. The same page says Part V of the Electoral Reform Act 2022 (powers over misinformation and disinformation) has not been commenced. Other parts of that Act were not section-checked. That uncommenced-law point is a gap, not a clearance.
- **Candidate data and photographs.** The Commission’s how-to-vote page says the ballot shows names, party emblems and candidate images. This hub will not copy names or images.
- **Data protection.** Ireland is in the EU, so the GDPR applies to personal data. The political-advertising page names the Data Protection Commission for targeting under the EU regulation. This hub publishes no personal data. The Data Protection Act 2018 was not section-checked.
- **Official sources.** electoralcommission.ie and voter.ie. A reuse licence for the Commission’s text was not read. The hub uses short original paraphrases and links.
- **Upcoming election.** The general-elections page says the most recent Dáil election was Friday 29 November 2024, that a general election has to take place at least every five years, and that after dissolution an election has to be held within 30 days. No next polling day was printed.
- **Verdict: onboard now.** English national hub only.

### Norway

- **Silence and polls.** The Directorate’s page on changes in the new election act says the updated § 21-8 does not allow publication of results before 21:00 on election day, including forecasts based on surveys carried out on election day or the day before. That is the Directorate’s summary. The Lovdata text of the section was not opened, so the citation is the Directorate page, not a confirmed Lovdata URL.
- **Candidate data.** The Storting page describes list proposals. This hub will not publish candidate names or photographs.
- **Data protection.** Norway is in the EEA, so the GDPR applies. Datatilsynet guidance was not read. This hub publishes no personal data.
- **Official sources.** valg.no page “Stortingsval”, published 21 May 2026. A reuse licence was not read.
- **Upcoming election.** That page says representatives are elected every four years in September and that the next Storting election is in September 2029. It does not name a day in 2029. The same page still walks through a calendar that names 2025 (age by the end of 2025, early voting from 1 July, advance voting from 11 August, election day Monday 8 September). Those dates are not treated as the 2029 timetable.
- **Verdict: onboard now.** English national hub only. No tracker, including around any future 21:00 rule, until a dated window is added from the statute.

### Sweden

- **Silence and polls.** No poll-publication ban was found on the Valmyndigheten English pages read on 23 October 2026 (the future-elections page, the right-to-vote page, and the distribution-of-seats page). Vallagen (2005:837) was not read section by section. That absence is not a finding that no ban exists. The hub still publishes no polls and no tracker.
- **Candidate data.** The distribution page describes personal votes. This hub will not name candidates or copy photographs. It also will not copy the 2026 party percentages.
- **Data protection.** Sweden is in the EU, so the GDPR applies. IMY guidance was not read. No personal data is published.
- **Official sources.** val.se. A reuse licence was not read.
- **Upcoming election.** The future-elections page, published 16 October 2026, says the next elections to the Riksdag and to regional and municipal councils are on Sunday 8 September 2030, and that these elections are held every four years on the second Sunday in September. The URL still contains “2026-elections”, and some FAQ headings on that page still say 2026. The hub follows the dated statement of 8 September 2030. The authority’s Riksdag result protocol records the previous election as 13 September 2026.
- **Verdict: onboard now.** English national hub only.

## Recommend only

### New Zealand

The Electoral Commission timetable lists the general election on Saturday 7 November 2026, advance voting from 26 October, and a regulated period from 7 August to 6 November 2026. Its advertising page says election advertising includes an advertisement that may reasonably be regarded as encouraging or persuading a vote, and that editorial content is not election advertising. Publishing a new election site while that regulated period is open is not a clear low-risk step, even if a neutral guide might later be argued to be editorial. A court decision sometimes cited on this advertising rule was not read. The Privacy Act 2020 was not read. No hub in this release.

### Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Portugal, Denmark, Finland, Czechia

Each is an EU member state, so the GDPR applies to any personal data. A modest official-source explainer may be possible later. It is not onboarded now because the national election statute was not read article by article on 23 October 2026, including any silence rule, any ban on publishing polls or candidate photographs, and the licence for official datasets. Secondary summaries, including a 2023 European Parliament briefing, were not treated as the current rule. No polling day was verified for this note, so none is stated.

## Skip

### Japan

Japan was removed from the site on 18 October 2026 together with other countries that had an unresolved election-law question. The Public Offices Election Act restricts campaign documents and related publication. The Act was not re-read article by article on 23 October 2026. Skip. Do not rebuild a hub on this note.

### South Korea

South Korea was removed on the same date. The Public Official Election Act is cited for a ban on publishing poll results in the days before election day through the close of polls. The article text was not re-read on 23 October 2026. Skip. Do not rebuild a hub on this note.

## What was onboarded from this note

Ireland, Norway and Sweden, as English national hubs: explainer, electoral system, official links, and an elections list. No municipality pages, no candidate or officeholder personal data, no photographs of people, and no tracker. Standing rules for those three, and for the United Kingdom (which already had pages but no dated window), are in `src/data/election-law.ts`. The build check requires every country card in the registry to have a dated window or one of those standing rules.
